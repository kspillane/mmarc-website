           SOTA:
             Bill Gerth, W4RK, reporting – We had a “SOTArama” in the Ozarks this past weekend,
             coinciding with the Ozarkcon QRP convention. Six or eight SOTA enthusiasts from Kansas,
             Arkansas, and Missouri. There were twenty five activations on nine different summits. It was
             quite a lot of activity. We also had a booth at the vendor exhibits at Ozarkcon and talked to a lot
             of interested people. If you would like to see some pictures, go to the website, www.mosota.org
             and at the bottom of the home page, there is a link to the pictures.

New Business:
             At the March meeting, Greg Abbott, WB0AQV, requested a report on the design status for radio at
             the new St. Mary’s Health Center. Bob Heald, W0XII reporting – there will be two radio rooms
             in the new facility: one in the patient tower, the other in the medical office building as backup.
             Cable runs will be installed and shielded. Antennas will likely be mounted on top of the six story
             patient tower.

              Kent Trimble, K9ZTV, reporting on Ozarkcon – There was no Titanic function this year. It was
              conducted for five years with the Nixa and Springfield clubs being involved. There was a lot of
              enthusiasm the first year, but very little in the following years. Several of our members went
              down and helped last year – they asked us to take it over and we declined. The only remaining
              Titanic operation is coming up soon at Pigeon Forge, TN. This was the 10th anniversary of
              Ozarkcon as a gathering of QRP enthusiasts. There was a Powerpoint presentation of the history
              of the event. There were about 140 attendees, many were designers and builders. Several
              worked QRP room to room on dummy loads. Technical advisor for telegraph in the movie
              “Lincoln” gave a presentation on the apparatus that was used in the movie. The movie is well
              worth seeing for a variety of reasons. The presentation was very interesting. I encourage
              everyone to attend at least once whether you are into QRP or not. They have a wide range of
              presentations and activities. It is the first weekend in April.

              Jack Boswell, N0KSF, reporting – We have a “projects” group called “Mid MO Technical
              Exchange Group” which meets once a month on the first Tuesday at 7:00 p.m. at the Red Cross
              Training Room. Everyone brings something they are working on and presents it as a show and
              tell, following with discussion about the project. Part of the purpose is trying to promote interest
              in the building and working of electronics.

Announcements:
        Roger Conway, KD0FAS, questions:
        a. Any progress on ARES Ids? Mike Dolson will check.
        b. Was there mention of using solar power at Field Day? Richard Manning, AC0SA, talked about a
           friend who has a solar array that he claimed could run quite a bit of the equipment. However,
           what he has would not be sufficient to power all the equipment we use. We do get points for any
           use of alternative energy, i.e.: charging a battery with a solar array then using that battery to
           power a rig on which five contacts are made.
        c. Any possibility of including the dates of upcoming Hamfests or other events in the minutes? The
